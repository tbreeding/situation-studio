# Framework lenses

Use these as bounded review perspectives. Apply published ideas accurately and sparingly. Never impersonate an author, imitate a distinctive voice, invent a quotation or endorsement, or call the result official, certified, or author-approved.

For framework-specific claims, distinguish the source principle from the reviewer's inference. Link to source material rather than reproducing chapters, worksheets, scripts, diagrams, podcast transcripts, or paid resources.

## Shared tests

Every reviewer must:

- cite the exact passage being assessed;
- preserve what already works;
- seek disconfirming evidence;
- distinguish a framework principle from stylistic preference;
- state when its lens is not the right primary tool;
- identify the cost of its recommendation;
- expose missing context and confidence;
- avoid assuming that preferred wording alone changes intent, power, or systems.

## Nonviolent Communication and power/request clarity

Source anchors:

- [Center for Nonviolent Communication chapter excerpt](https://www.nonviolentcommunication.com/pdf_files/nvc2-chapter-one.html)
- [CNVC key distinctions](https://www.cnvc.org/images/pdf/certification/CPP-Version-Nov-2016-A4-format-English.pdf)

Test:

- Could a camera or recorder capture the observation, or is it evaluation?
- Are stated feelings emotions rather than interpretations of another person's intent?
- Is a need a broadly human value, or a strategy prescribing what a specific person must do?
- Is a request concrete, positive, doable, and genuinely open to dialogue?
- Does “I need you to…” hide a demand or role requirement?
- Has the manager listened for the other person's observations, feelings, needs, and request?
- If something is non-negotiable, is it named honestly as a decision, expectation, boundary, or requirement?

Watch for pseudo-observations, moral judgment, requests with unstated punishment, formulaic NVC language, and compassion used to avoid legitimate accountability. Reconcile by identifying needs internally, listening empathically, and stating organizational requirements separately and transparently.

## Tactical empathy and negotiation

Source anchors:

- [Black Swan negotiation guide](https://www.blackswanltd.com/never-split-the-difference-negotiating-contracts)
- [Black Swan concept updates](https://www.blackswanltd.com/newsletter/expert-negotiator-concepts-that-have-evolved-since-never-split-the-difference-was-published)

Test:

- What fear, loss, status concern, negative interpretation, or constraint may need acknowledgment?
- Are labels tentative and corrigible rather than assertions about an inner state?
- Would a mirror, summary, or calibrated what/how question reveal material information?
- Does the other person feel understood rather than maneuvered toward agreement?
- What hidden stakeholder or implementation constraint could break the agreement?
- Is this actually negotiable, or is it feedback, coaching, discipline, or a fixed decision?

Watch for cargo-cult labels, weaponized questions, cornering sequences, adversarial framing, and tactics that conceal authority. Use this lens primarily for de-escalation, discovery, resistance, and implementation—not covert control.

## Coaching and curiosity

Source anchors:

- [Michael Bungay Stanier coaching skills](https://www.mbs.works/coaching-skills/)
- [The Advice Trap overview](https://www.mbs.works/advice-trap-book/)

Test:

- Is there meaningful room for the employee to choose, or does the manager owe feedback, instruction, or a decision?
- Has the manager explored the actual challenge, desired outcome, and useful support?
- Can the employee generate options before the manager gives advice?
- Are tradeoffs visible?
- Are questions short, singular, and followed by real listening?
- Does the exchange close with ownership and learning?

Watch for premature advice, advice disguised as a question, coaching theater after a decision is closed, interrogation, and curiosity without a boundary or next action. Name the conversational mode: explore where autonomy is real and state expectations where it is not.

## Team health and organizational clarity

Source anchors:

- [The Table Group Five Dysfunctions summary](https://files.tablegroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/11224029/FiveDysfunctions.pdf)
- [The Table Group organizational health overview](https://www.tablegroup.com/topics-and-resources/organizational-health/)

Test:

- Does the guidance build vulnerability-based trust and allow substantive dissent?
- Does it distinguish productive conflict from personal attack or work-blocking behavior?
- Was there a fair opportunity for conflict before commitment was expected?
- Are the decision, rationale, priority, behavior standard, owner, and decision rights clear?
- Can peers share accountability, or has it all been centralized in the manager?
- Is collective performance affected?
- Is an individual issue partly a missing norm, unclear process, or unreinforced expectation?

Watch for artificial harmony, consensus as a prerequisite, commitment before fair disagreement, vague “team player” conformity tests, tolerated destructive high performance, and one-time clarity without reinforcement. Invite conflict before closure, state closure explicitly, and then hold observable commitments while repairing the operating system.

## Care plus direct challenge

Source anchors:

- [Radical Candor official overview](https://www.radicalcandor.com/for-you)
- [Radical Candor one-page guide](https://www.radicalcandor.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Radical-Candor-One-Pager.pdf)

Test:

- Are personal care and direct challenge both present?
- Is criticism specific, humble, useful, timely, and about behavior rather than identity?
- Is praise sincere and specific rather than cushioning or leverage?
- Is criticism private where practicable?
- Does the manager invite correction of their own interpretation and notice how the message lands?
- Would vagueness leave the person unable to improve?

Watch for kindness without clarity, bluntness without care, convenient flattery, “candor” as permission for cruelty, and impact dismissed because intent was good. Care through relationship and listening; challenge through behavior-based truth and transparent expectations.

## Systems, upstream prevention, and behavior change

Source anchors:

- [Switch chapter one](https://heathbrothers.com/switch-chapter-1/)
- [Upstream one-page summary](https://danheath.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Upstream-by-Dan-Heath-1-page-summary-1.pdf)
- [Reset one-page summary](https://danheath.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Reset-One-Pager.pdf)

Keep the three tests distinct:

- Change: clarify direction, engage motivation, and shape the environment.
- Prevention: overcome problem blindness, ownership gaps, and tunneling; find leverage and early warning.
- Reset: observe the work, clarify the goal behind the goal, find bright spots, target the constraint, map the system, and reallocate resources.

Ask:

- Does apparent resistance reflect unclear direction, exhaustion, friction, or environment?
- What critical move and destination must become visible?
- Where is a bright spot or earlier signal?
- What recurring harm could be prevented upstream?
- Who owns the system, what is the main constraint, and what leverage point matters?
- What capacity, resource, process, incentive, or decision right must change?
- What measure would show progress and what paired measure would reveal side effects?

Watch for polishing a conversation while leaving causes untouched, adding expectations without capacity, launching many improvements instead of addressing the constraint, treating activity as success, and using “the system” to erase personal agency. Pair the conversation with a system check while retaining accountability only for behavior within the person's control.

## Management operating cadence and behavioral feedback

Source anchors:

- [Manager Tools Basics](https://www.manager-tools.com/manager-tools-basics)
- [Manager Tools feedback model resource](https://www.manager-tools.com/system/files/documents/Manager_Tools_Feedback_Model.pdf)

Respect the repository's originality and licensing rules. Treat Manager Tools as an influence or learning source, not an affiliation. Do not transplant its scripts, branded instructional assets, or long-form model explanations into public pages.

Test:

- Is the issue an observable behavior rather than attitude, intention, or character?
- Is the impact concrete and proportionate?
- Is this a brief feedback moment, skill coaching, delegation, a one-on-one topic, or formal corrective action?
- Does the employee have a real opportunity to propose future behavior where appropriate?
- Is any request for permission meaningful at least as to timing?
- Will the manager follow up through ordinary cadence and reinforce effective behavior as well as correct misses?

Watch for batched indictments, speculative impact, prosecutorial why-questions, overprescription, feedback as punishment, mechanical formula use, hollow permission under hierarchy, and confusing feedback with coaching or discipline. Keep feedback behavioral and future-oriented while naming non-negotiable role expectations separately.

## Recurring cross-framework tensions

Use these to create the issue register:

- relational empathy versus tactical influence;
- genuine request versus transparent managerial requirement;
- exploratory coaching versus owed feedback or direction;
- productive dissent versus commitment after a decision;
- personal care versus unmistakable challenge;
- individual agency versus system causes and capacity;
- concise feedback versus adequate discovery;
- immediate correction versus upstream prevention;
- natural speech versus formula fidelity.

Seek an explicit tradeoff and coherent resolution. Do not solve tensions by stacking every technique into one speech.
